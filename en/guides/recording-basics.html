Overdubbing: the essentials before you record

Overdubbing adds one track at a time, but your first recording becomes the reference for everything that follows. A few minutes of preparation can make a big difference.

1. Establish a tempo reference

A click or steady rhythm makes it easier to add other instruments later. You can also record the first part without a fixed tempo, but the later parts will need to follow its timing changes.

2. Leave headroom at the input

If the recording level is too high, louder moments may distort. Recording too quietly has disadvantages too, but avoiding clipping comes first in digital recording. Test the loudest part before making the take.

3. Pay attention to monitoring latency

Hearing your own performance with a delay can make it harder to stay in time. Latency varies with the device, app and equipment such as Bluetooth headphones. Choose a low-latency monitoring method for overdubbing where possible.

4. Check each take immediately

A performance may feel good while still containing noise, clipping or a problem with its start position. Play back a short section before continuing a long session.

5. Keep original takes and finished versions separate

Preserve the original recordings as you edit and export. Distinguishing original audio, work in progress and finished versions makes it easier to go back later.
